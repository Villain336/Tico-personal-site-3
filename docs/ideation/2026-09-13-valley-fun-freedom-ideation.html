Grounding Context

Codebase Context

Shalom Valley is a Phaser world with a React overlay. A day is 240 seconds; a night is 120. Dawn already settles the ledger, civic offices, judgment, and stranger arrivals. Dusk always replaces the job ribbon with the same three combat chores. Seven panels already exist: Build, Skills, Roster, Quests, Books, Gear, Civic. Another panel is the failure mode this pass is trying to avoid.

Player writing exists but is thin: a character name; up to eight laws of 140 characters that compile through keyword lists; a Book that stores soul kinds (arrived, fallen, redeemed, mercy, fine, exile, lost) and a dawn letter that quotes the last line. Villagers have seed looks, a name from a 28-name pool, and fixed mood lines. On pass-by they quote a law 75% of the time. They have no wants, relationships, or memory.

David, Noah, Moses, and Paul arrive on calendar days with kill, harvest, or redeem counters. Signs (drought, long night, quiet dawn) are weather multipliers after day 4. Civic offices bump numbers. There is one tunic recipe. Beasts wander. Interiors are pocket rooms with one prop. Save version is 11. The Phaser–React bridge and the map seed stay. God is never a unit.

External Context

The research split that matters: authorship play (the game as a storytelling partner) versus participation play (experiencing a designer plot). Small named casts plus relationship labels beat extra quest panels. Dual loops feel alive only when each half changes the other’s option space (Recettear), and hollow when they do not (Moonlighter). More meters do not create depth. Faith-adjacent games that stay playful do it through craft and humour, not virtue dashboards.

Topic Axes

  1. Daily loop — what your hands do in a 360-second cycle
  2. Village as people — souls, the Book, relationships
  3. Creative expression — player authorship that the world can quote
  4. World texture — discovery on a fixed 16×16 map
  5. Freedom vs rails — quests, eras, and gates

The Book as partner

Description
Stop treating the Book as a receipt printer. At dawn, sift yesterday into one causal sentence — because this law, this raid, this named fall — and let the player edit or strike that sentence on the existing Books panel or at the altar. The dawn letter quotes the player’s line. Soul entries gain one witness: another name or a landmark. Villagers stay simple; the Book does the remembering.
Axis
Village as people (souls, Book, relationships)
Basis
direct: quoteBook only templates the last soul’s kind (“who fell / was brought back”). The Books panel is a read-only list; SoulEntry.note is system-written. Pass-by speech is a law quote or a greet pool — nobody remembers anybody.
external: WAWLT’s editable transcript (simulation proposes, player writes the canon); RimWorld apophenia from two-line reasons; parish registers that reconstruct households from sparse pairs.
Rationale
This is the only existing artifact the design already says letters quote. Making it a partner — not a log — turns named souls into tomorrow’s vocabulary without a new HUD tab. It is authorship play using L and the dawn letter you already open.
Downsides
Sifting badly becomes fortune-cookie lore. Editing at dawn can feel like homework if the draft is dull. A witness field needs a save bump (v12).

2 Daily loop Conf High Cx Medium

The night that elects the law

Description
Dusk stops wiping the day’s ribbon. Named officers and written laws can staff “hold the wall” so the sword is a temptation, not the only night. At dawn, the night just survived marks exactly one already-written law as in force until the next dusk. The others stay on the tablet and in mouths; only the elected law feeds civic math and court. A drought can elect “share the bread”; a long night can elect curfew.
Axis
Daily loop / what you do with your hands
Basis
direct: enterDusk() replaces the ribbon with the same three combat jobs regardless of the day’s letter. hasIntent ORs every written law at once, so eight tablet slots are a stacked buff, not a deck. civicDawnMods only emits rent, spawn, wage, and interest numbers.
external: Recettear’s halves must change each other’s option space; Moonlighter’s combat half barely teaches the shop. Cult of the Lamb made the violent option work.
Rationale
Night is every cycle’s climax, and it currently throws away the day’s authorship. Electing a law makes hands-on night play compile tomorrow without a new meter. Writing laws becomes choosing a deck the night can draw from.
Downsides
Players who only fight may never see their prose “win.” Election rules can feel arbitrary if the sift is opaque. Skipping dusk overwrite without staffing the wall can make nights too easy.
Confidence
80%

3 Freedom vs rails Conf High Cx Medium

Strangers read the tablet

Description
Keep the famous arrivals if you want the calendar spine, but compile each stranger’s ask from the Book, the elected law, and last night’s kind — the same keyword compiler laws already use. Kill-eight-robbers can become “redeem the three who fell under your curfew.” A stronger variant: they arrive when a condition is ripe (a fold, a fallen name, a sign), not when a day index ticks. The journal stays J; it lists a spoken want instead of “N dawns.”
Axis
Freedom vs rails (quests, eras, gates)
Basis
direct: David / Noah / Moses / Paul are arrivesDay 2 / 3 / 5 / 7 with fixed killCount, harvestCount, and redeemCount. The prologue promises each has a story and needs something from you first. Holy Ghost and Jesus already refuse the clock.
external: Authorship versus participation play (Kreminski & Mateas). Recettear gates feel dramatic because they read the other half of the run.
Rationale
The railroad is not that strangers come. It is that their homework ignores everything you authored. One recruit who reads the tablet proves the rest do not have to be patriarch skins on a progress bar.
Downsides
Generated asks can miss the biblical beat that made the name worth shipping. Ripe-condition timing is harder to tutorial than “day 2.”
Confidence
78%

A household of five

Description
Cap living named souls at about five. Offices, letters, falls, mercy, and recruit-titles attach to those people. Newcomers stay unnamed until a name frees. Appointing Miriam is the watchman: she walks the dusk job, her pass-by line changes, the Book records the seat. Houses stop being empty pocket rooms — the people who belong there are inside.
Axis
Village as people (souls, Book, relationships)
Basis
direct: SOUL_NAMES has 28 entries; BOOK_CAP is 40. setOffice already binds a villager seed, but office copy is a spawn / loyalty / interest bump. Interiors are a floor plus one prop; nobody is home.
external: RimWorld’s ~12-colonist cap so players know individuals; Twilight Bazaar’s fewer, simpler traces; Quiet Year’s community zoom.
Rationale
Apophenia dies in a crowd. A household the player can actually hold in mind makes laws, offices, and rooms mean something. It is the opposite of arrive-fall-forget.
Downsides
A hard cap fights the “strangers at dawn” fantasy and may need a save bump plus spawn-rate retune. Occupied interiors are a second project hiding inside the first. Five can feel lonely if nights still spawn anonymous shadows.
Confidence
62%

5 Creative expression Conf Med Cx Medium

Temptation that works

Description
Let a cruel efficiency pay now and invoice later. Open gates already buy rent and extra shadows. Compound that: travelers who arrive while gates are open are written as named souls. If you later elect curfew or close the gates, some of those same names return as edge-walkers or as the accused. Hunting for a quick pelt can thin the flock and make the next drought likelier. The short-term win is real. The Book collects the bill. God stays a voice, never a unit.
Axis
Creative expression / player authorship
Basis
direct: Open-gates copy already admits the tradeoff: more travelers and rent, and more enemies at the edge. Spawn today feeds anonymous night enemies, not named travelers. Hunt cases already file only if a no-hunt or kind-to-beasts law is compiled.
external: Cult of the Lamb made sacrifice tempting with a real power upgrade, so moral authorship stayed player-owned.
Rationale
Authorship is not typing a nicer law. It is inviting bodies, taking the gold, and living with the names. Mercy versus order becomes a story you can retell without a virtue meter.
Downsides
If the invoice is delayed too long, it reads as random punishment. If it is too fast, players never take the bait. Named returning enemies need a small identity hook so they are not just reskins.
Confidence
68%

6 World texture Conf High Cx Low

Walk the sign

Description
Keep the three signs and their grow / thirst / spawn math. Stop announcing them as HUD weather. Discover drought as hard ground and thirsty wells, long night as a dusk that will not lift, quiet dawn as empty roads. Optional second beat: the sign acts as a lens over the tablet (drought asks about open hands; long night asks about curfew). Discovery is attention on the tiles you already have.
Axis
World texture / discovery
Basis
direct: Sign copy already wants to be walked (“The ground hardens…”, “The dark stays…”). pickSign refuses a sign before day 4 and refuses two in a row. The numbers exist; the tell is a toast and a HUD chip.
external: Eastward’s freedom as living in the world; The Serpent & The Seed leading with craft and wonder rather than virtue UI.
Rationale
This is the cheapest texture win on a locked map seed. It makes the divine surface something you inhabit, not a banner, and it stays off any new panel.
Downsides
Players who ignore wells may miss the sign entirely unless some existing toast remains as a whisper. A “lens” on top can balloon into idea 2 if both ship at once.
Confidence
82%

7 Freedom vs rails Conf Med Cx Medium

After the weighing, the middle continues

Description
The first verdict is not the credits. Blessing, exile, or dark reset permanently tilts the compiler and then drops the climax spine: blessing quietly keeps a sanctuary-like intent in force; exile strikes a law and keeps those names; dark reset leaves a standing scar the letter can quote. Play continues as tending, writing, and sifting. No second boss. No second weighing. God remains weather and a voice.
Axis
Freedom vs rails (quests, eras, gates)
Basis
direct: canCallJudgment already says the valley has been weighed. After a verdict, pickSign returns none and compileLaw ignores the outcome — it is a stop, not a tilt.
external: Stardew’s never-ending middle; The Quiet Year’s refusal of an epilogue score. The analogy is the desire for a middle, not a claim that the current verdict already provides one.
Rationale
The stacked eras march toward a single weighing. If that weighing retunes the valley, the work already shipped starts paying rent instead of emptying the loop.
Downsides
A tilted compiler can feel like a hidden new game the player did not opt into. Needs a clear “the trial is over; the village remains” beat so it does not read as a bug. Weaker until ideas 1 or 2 exist to give the middle something to tend.
Confidence
60%
Complexity
